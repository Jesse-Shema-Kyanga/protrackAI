This project addresses operational inefficiencies in employee productivity management at MTN Rwanda by developing ProTrackAI, an intelligent digital solution to monitor, classify, and optimize workforce engagement. Current manual oversight methods fail to distinguish productive digital activity from distractions, leading to time wastage and suboptimal performance.
ProTrackAI solves these challenges through AI-driven activity classification, real-time productivity tracking, and comprehensive analytics dashboards. The system employs a lightweight desktop agent to monitor application usage and web activity, transmitting data to a centralized backend for intelligent analysis using TensorFlow.js and scikit-learn.
The system is developed using React with Material UI for frontend interfaces, Node.js and Express for backend services, and MongoDB for data management. Key features include automated activity classification, detailed productivity reports, employee self-evaluation portals, and goal management tools. By providing data-driven insights into digital work patterns, ProTrackAI aims to enhance efficiency, establish accountability, and support informed HR decisions.
Through intelligent monitoring and actionable analytics, ProTrackAI enables organizations to reduce time wastage, optimize resource allocation, and improve overall workforce productivity. The solution is designed to be privacy-conscious and scalable, supporting business growth in Rwanda's digital economy.

CHAPTER 1
GENERAL INTRODUCTION
Introduction
The contemporary workplace has undergone a dramatic transformation with the widespread adoption of digital technologies and remote work models. While these changes have introduced unprecedented flexibility and connectivity, they have simultaneously created new challenges in measuring and maintaining employee productivity. Organizations increasingly struggle to distinguish genuine work engagement from digital distractions, particularly as employees spend the majority of their workday interacting with computers and online platforms.
In Rwanda's rapidly evolving digital economy, companies like MTN Rwanda face mounting pressure to optimize workforce productivity while maintaining service quality standards. The absence of intelligent monitoring systems means managers often rely on subjective assessments rather than objective data when evaluating employee performance. This gap between technological capability and management practice creates opportunities for substantial productivity losses that directly affect organizational competitiveness and profitability.
Traditional approaches to workplace monitoring have proven inadequate for modern digital work environments. Basic network filtering systems can block access to inappropriate websites, but they cannot differentiate between productive and non-productive use of permitted platforms. An employee might spend hours on YouTube—watching either work-related tutorials or entertainment content—yet current monitoring tools treat these activities identically. This lack of contextual understanding leaves managers without the granular insights needed to support struggling employees or recognize high performers.
This research addresses these challenges by developing ProTrackAI, an artificial intelligence-powered productivity management system designed specifically for the Rwandan workplace context. By combining real-time activity monitoring with machine learning classification algorithms, the system provides organizations with the objective data needed to optimize workforce performance while creating transparency and accountability frameworks that benefit both employers and employees.






Background of the Study
The telecommunications sector in Rwanda has experienced remarkable growth over the past decade, with companies like MTN Rwanda serving millions of subscribers across the country. As one of the leading mobile network operators, MTN Rwanda employs a substantial workforce responsible for customer service, network operations, technical support, and administrative functions. The nature of these roles requires employees to spend the majority of their workday using computers and accessing internet resources, creating an environment where digital productivity becomes a critical factor in organizational performance.
Despite the digital-intensive nature of modern telecommunications work, many organizations continue to rely on outdated approaches to workplace oversight. During a two-month internship at MTN Rwanda, direct observation revealed significant gaps in how employee digital activity was managed and monitored. Upon joining the organization, employees were provided with portal accounts that served as gateways to internet access. This system displayed warnings about prohibited content and showed countdown timers indicating when internet access would expire, creating the impression of comprehensive monitoring. However, the reality proved far less sophisticated.
The existing system operated primarily as a content filter rather than a productivity management tool. A single staff member was responsible for overseeing internet usage across the organization, with the system's main function being the blocking of websites deemed inappropriate or shameful. This blacklist-based approach meant that employees could access any non-blacklisted website without scrutiny or accountability. While administrators could theoretically view browsing history, there was no active monitoring of how employees spent their digital time. Throughout the two-month observation period, not a single employee was called out or counseled regarding their internet usage patterns, regardless of how much time they spent on non-work-related activities.
The implications of this hands-off approach became apparent through daily observation. Employees frequently accessed social media platforms like Instagram and video-sharing sites like YouTube throughout the workday. The system made no distinction between an employee watching a technical tutorial relevant to their role and one watching entertainment content. More critically, there was no mechanism to identify employees who spent entire days engaged in minimal productive work. Personal productivity became entirely self-regulated, with no objective data to inform performance evaluations or identify employees who might need additional support or guidance.
This lack of intelligent oversight created an environment where procrastination went undetected and work quality suffered due to last-minute rushes to meet deadlines. High-performing employees received no recognition for their focused work habits, while underperforming employees faced no accountability for time wastage. Managers lacked the data needed to understand productivity patterns, identify training needs, or make informed decisions about resource allocation. The organization was essentially operating blind, unable to distinguish between employees who were genuinely engaged in their work and those who were merely present online.
The contrast between MTN Rwanda's technological sophistication as a telecommunications provider and its rudimentary approach to workforce productivity management highlighted a critical gap in organizational tooling. What was needed was not merely stricter internet restrictions—which could stifle legitimate research and learning—but rather an intelligent system capable of understanding the context and productivity value of digital activities. Such a system would need to balance oversight with employee autonomy, provide actionable insights to management, and create transparency that benefits both the organization and its workforce.
Recognizing these challenges and their impact on organizational efficiency, this research proposes the development of ProTrackAI, an artificial intelligence-powered employee productivity management system. Unlike simple content filters, ProTrackAI employs machine learning algorithms to classify digital activities based on their productive value, providing managers with nuanced insights while respecting employee privacy. By addressing the specific limitations observed at MTN Rwanda, this system aims to transform how organizations monitor, understand, and optimize workforce productivity in digital work environments.
Statement of the Problem
MTN Rwanda currently operates with a monitoring system that fails to address employee productivity management in digital work environments. The existing portal-based internet access system functions solely as a content filter, blocking inappropriate websites while providing no meaningful insights into how employees utilize their work time.
The system cannot distinguish between productive and non-productive use of permitted websites. An employee spending hours on YouTube watching entertainment is treated identically to one researching technical solutions. This lack of contextual understanding means substantial paid work time may be consumed by non-work activities without detection.The reliance on a single staff member to oversee internet usage across the organization is neither scalable nor effective. With no active monitoring or automated alerts, productivity patterns go untracked and time-wasting behaviors remain unidentified. Managers lack objective data to inform performance evaluations, identify training needs, or recognize high performers.
The consequences are tangible: work quality suffers as tasks are rushed at deadlines, the organization cannot assess workforce efficiency, and service delivery is compromised. Without intelligent monitoring, MTN Rwanda lacks crucial visibility into how its human capital investment translates into productive output.

Objectives of the Study
This research aims to develop an intelligent productivity management system that addresses the limitations observed in current monitoring approaches, providing organizations with actionable insights while maintaining employee privacy and fostering accountability.
General Objective
To develop an intelligent system that enhances employee performance optimization and facilitates data-driven workforce management by providing comprehensive insights into productivity, time management, and performance trends.
Specific Objectives
* To accurately track and classify employee activity across web and desktop applications during office hours, distinguishing between productive and non-productive engagement.
* To generate detailed, quantifiable reports on individual, team, and departmental productivity, highlighting task completion rates, time allocation, and performance trends.
* To establish a transparent framework for goal setting, progress monitoring, and performance evaluation, fostering accountability and continuous improvement.

* To implement hierarchical monitoring capabilities that allow appropriate stakeholders (Supervisors, HR) to monitor employee and team activity based on their respective roles and permissions.


Scope of the Study
This study focuses on developing and implementing ProTrackAI, an intelligent employee productivity management system specifically designed to address digital workplace monitoring challenges observed at MTN Rwanda. The research encompasses the design, development, and validation of a system that leverages artificial intelligence to classify employee digital activities and provide actionable productivity insights.
Included in the Scope:
The system development will incorporate AI-powered classification of web and desktop activities as productive or non-productive, utilizing machine learning algorithms to provide contextual understanding beyond simple blacklist filtering. The platform will generate comprehensive productivity reports at daily, weekly, and monthly intervals, detailing employee activity patterns, time utilization metrics, and performance trends. Hierarchical monitoring capabilities will enable supervisors and HR personnel to access relevant productivity data based on their organizational roles and permissions.
Core functionality includes real-time activity monitoring that tracks active application usage, window titles, and URLs on employee desktop devices during designated work hours. The system will implement user management features for secure login, profile management, and role-based access control. Goal management tools will allow employees and supervisors to set, track, and evaluate performance objectives. An employee self-evaluation portal will provide workers with insights into their own productivity patterns and facilitate feedback mechanisms. Time and attendance monitoring will track check-in and check-out times, displaying attendance trends and generating alerts for irregularities.
Excluded from the Scope:
The system will not incorporate physical presence tracking technologies such as GPS monitoring, biometric entry systems, or location-based surveillance. Direct content analysis is explicitly excluded—the AI will not examine or store actual document contents, email text, or private communications. Monitoring is limited to application metadata, window titles, and URLs without accessing the substance of employee work products.
Real-time employee interventions are outside the project scope; the system provides data for management review rather than automatically triggering alerts or disciplinary actions directed at employees. Integration with complex HR systems including payroll processing, benefits administration, or enterprise HRIS platforms is not included. Advanced project management features such as in-application Gantt chart creation, resource allocation tools, or project budgeting capabilities are excluded.
The system will not automate disciplinary actions or reward mechanisms based solely on productivity metrics—these decisions remain human management functions. Activity monitoring is limited to desktop operating systems and will not extend to personal mobile devices. The project does not include development of adaptive AI model retraining mechanisms that continuously learn from user feedback within the live system; classification relies on pre-trained models developed during the implementation phase.
The research will be conducted through collaboration with MTN Rwanda as the case study organization, focusing specifically on productivity management for desk-based roles. Data collection will involve system testing with simulated scenarios and validation of classification accuracy against manually labeled activity samples. The study timeline spans the academic year, with system development occurring over four months and validation testing conducted over six weeks.


Methodology and Techniques Used in the Study
This research employed direct observation and informal interviews as primary methods to gather data on current productivity monitoring practices at MTN Rwanda and to inform the design of ProTrackAI.
Observation
Direct observation served as the foundational research method for this study. Observation is a systematic data collection technique where the researcher directly witnesses and records behaviors, activities, and phenomena in their natural setting without manipulation or intervention. This method is particularly valuable for understanding workplace dynamics and identifying gaps between stated policies and actual practices.
During a two-month internship at MTN Rwanda, systematic observation was conducted of the organization's internet monitoring and productivity management practices. This extended period allowed for comprehensive understanding of daily operational patterns, employee digital behavior, and the effectiveness of existing oversight mechanisms.
Key Observations from the Internship:
Upon arrival, employees were provided portal accounts to access the internet. The portal displayed warnings about appropriate internet usage and showed countdown timers for internet access expiration. This created the impression of comprehensive monitoring. However, observation revealed the system functioned with minimal active oversight, with a single staff member responsible for the monitoring infrastructure across the organization.
When inquiring about the system's capabilities, the internship supervisor clarified that while the system technically possesses website blocking functionality, stating "it can but I don't think it does," this feature is not actively utilized in practice. Throughout the entire two-month observation period, this was confirmed—no websites were ever blocked, and employees experienced completely unrestricted internet access. Employees could freely access social media platforms like Instagram, video-sharing sites like YouTube, news websites, entertainment portals, and any other internet content without encountering restrictions.
The monitoring approach was entirely passive with no productivity analysis. The system logged which websites employees visited but made no distinction between productive and non-productive use of any sites. There was no tracking of time spent on different platforms, no classification of content types, and no analysis of browsing patterns. Throughout the observation period, no employee was ever questioned or counseled about their internet usage patterns, regardless of how much time they spent on non-work-related activities.
Personal experience within this environment revealed how the absence of active monitoring affected work habits. Without accountability mechanisms, productivity became entirely self-regulated. It was possible to spend substantial portions of the workday on non-work activities—watching YouTube videos, browsing social media, or engaging in personal research—without any oversight. The system provided no feedback on time allocation or work focus.
This lack of oversight led to procrastination patterns where tasks were frequently completed in rushed, last-minute efforts as deadlines approached. The absence of objective productivity metrics meant that employees who maintained strong work discipline received no recognition, while those who engaged in extensive time-wasting faced no consequences. Supervisors had no data-driven insights into how their team members spent digital work time, relying instead on output-based assessments that could not capture underlying productivity issues.
Interview
An informal interview was conducted with a colleague to clarify the actual policies and enforcement mechanisms of the monitoring system. Interviews are a qualitative research method involving direct conversation with participants to gather detailed information about their experiences, knowledge, and perspectives on a particular topic.
Key Characteristics of Research Interviews:
* Qualitative focus capturing rich, detailed information about experiences and perspectives
* Flexible format allowing researchers to probe deeper into relevant topics as they emerge
* Enables participants to explain the reasoning behind behaviors or policies
* Provides validation for observational findings through direct confirmation from knowledgeable sources
Types of Interviews:
1. Structured Interviews: Follow predetermined questions with no deviation, ensuring consistency across participants
2. Semi-Structured Interviews: Use a prepared question guide but allow flexibility to explore emerging themes
3. Unstructured Interviews: Conversational approach with minimal predetermined structure, useful for exploratory research
For this study, an unstructured interview approach was employed through informal conversations with colleagues to clarify monitoring system policies and practices.


Interview Process:
An informal interview was conducted with a colleague who had extensive experience with the organization's internet monitoring system. When asked about internet usage restrictions and monitoring practices, the colleague confirmed that employees faced no website restrictions in practice. She explained that "anything was fine as long as you weren't going on terrible sites," but clarified this was more of a general professional expectation rather than an enforced technical restriction.

The interview provided important confirmation that the observed lack of productivity monitoring was not merely a temporary gap but rather the standard operational approach, helping establish that ProTrackAI would address a genuine organizational need.

Systems analysis is a critical discipline in information technology that focuses on understanding and evaluating existing processes, identifying inefficiencies, and designing improved solutions. It involves the systematic examination of how organizations operate, with particular emphasis on the flow of information, resource utilization, and the achievement of business objectives. Through structured analysis techniques, researchers can identify gaps between current practices and desired outcomes, providing the foundation for effective system design and implementation.
In the context of employee productivity management, systems analysis becomes essential for understanding how organizations monitor, measure, and optimize workforce performance. Traditional approaches to workplace oversight often fail to leverage available technological capabilities, resulting in missed opportunities for efficiency improvements and data-driven decision-making. By conducting thorough analysis of existing systems, researchers can identify specific pain points, quantify their impact, and propose targeted solutions that address root causes rather than symptoms.
The importance of systems analysis extends beyond mere problem identification. It provides a structured framework for documenting current processes, establishing baseline performance metrics, and justifying investment in new technologies. This documentation becomes crucial for organizational learning, enabling stakeholders to understand not only what problems exist but why they persist and how proposed solutions will deliver measurable improvements.
For this research, systems analysis served as the foundation for understanding MTN Rwanda's approach to employee productivity monitoring. Through systematic observation and evaluation documented in Chapter 1, significant gaps were identified between the organization's technological capabilities and its actual monitoring practices.
To build an intelligent productivity management solution, this chapter examines the existing monitoring infrastructure at MTN Rwanda through the following dimensions:
1. Organizational context of MTN Rwanda including its operational background and strategic objectives
2. Current monitoring infrastructure describing the portal-based system and its operational mechanisms
3. Process modeling visualizing how the existing system functions through AS-IS diagrams

This comprehensive evaluation establishes the baseline understanding necessary to design a system that resolves identified gaps while meeting organizational needs for productivity optimization and accountability.

MTN Rwanda operates as one of the leading telecommunications providers in the country, serving millions of subscribers with mobile voice, data, and digital services. As a major player in Rwanda's digital economy, the organization employs a substantial workforce across customer service, network operations, technical support, and administrative functions. The nature of MTN Rwanda's operations requires employees to spend significant portions of their workday using computers and accessing internet resources for customer support, technical troubleshooting, system monitoring, and administrative tasks. This digital-intensive work environment creates inherent challenges in distinguishing productive digital engagement from time-wasting activities. Despite the organization's technological sophistication in delivering telecommunications services, its internal approach to workforce productivity monitoring has remained relatively rudimentary, relying primarily on content filtering rather than intelligent activity analysis.

Description of Current System
The current employee internet monitoring system at MTN Rwanda operates through a portal-based access control mechanism that serves as the gateway for all employee internet connectivity. Upon joining the organization, employees receive individual portal accounts with unique credentials that must be used to authenticate and gain access to the company's internet infrastructure.
When employees log into the portal, the system displays warnings about appropriate professional internet usage and features countdown timers that show the remaining duration of internet access before re-authentication is required. This visual presentation creates an impression of comprehensive oversight and active monitoring, suggesting that employee digital activity is being tracked and potentially restricted based on content or usage patterns.
However, the actual functionality of the system is considerably more limited than its interface implies. While the system architecture includes technical capability for website blocking and content filtering, this functionality is not actively deployed. When discussing the monitoring infrastructure, the internship supervisor noted that the system "can but I don't think it does" block websites, indicating that while the capability exists within the system, it is not utilized in practice. Throughout the two-month observation period, this was confirmed through direct experience—no websites were ever blocked, and employees had completely unrestricted access to the entire internet.
The monitoring infrastructure operates as a passive logging system rather than an active productivity management or content control tool. The system records which websites employees visit and when they access them, but provides no analysis, restrictions, or interventions based on this data. Employees have unrestricted access to social media platforms like Instagram and Facebook, video-sharing sites like YouTube, news portals, entertainment websites, and any other internet content without encountering blocks or warnings.
A single staff member is designated to oversee the internet monitoring system across the entire organization. This individual's responsibilities focus primarily on maintaining system functionality and responding to technical access issues. There is no systematic analysis of browsing patterns, time allocation, or productivity metrics derived from the logged data. The system technically captures comprehensive logs of internet activity, but these logs are not actively reviewed or analyzed for productivity insights.
The system makes no distinction between different types of internet usage. An employee watching a work-related technical tutorial on YouTube is logged identically to one watching entertainment videos. Time spent on LinkedIn for professional networking is recorded the same way as time spent on Instagram browsing personal content. Without contextual understanding or analysis, the logged data provides no actionable intelligence about employee productivity patterns.
Throughout the typical workday, employees experience no interventions or feedback regarding their internet usage. There are no alerts for extended periods on any websites, no notifications about visiting non-work-related sites, and no automated reports sent to supervisors about team members' browsing patterns. Productivity is entirely self-regulated, with employees personally responsible for allocating their time between productive work and digital distractions without external accountability mechanisms.
Performance evaluations and productivity assessments occur through traditional output-based metrics rather than digital activity analysis. Managers evaluate employees based on completed tasks, project deliverables, customer service metrics, and subjective observations of engagement. Unified Modelling Language (UML)
Unified Modeling Language (UML) is a standardized visual modeling notation used extensively in software engineering to represent system designs, architectures, and behaviors. Developed in the mid-1990s and maintained by the Object Management Group (OMG), UML provides a common language for software developers, system architects, business analysts, and stakeholders to communicate about complex systems without ambiguity.
Purpose and Benefits
UML serves multiple critical functions in software development:
Visualization: Complex system architectures and component interactions become comprehensible through visual diagrams. Rather than parsing thousands of lines of descriptive text, stakeholders can quickly understand system structure and behavior through intuitive graphical representations.
Specification: UML diagrams serve as precise specifications for system functionality. Developers can reference these models during implementation to ensure the code aligns with design intent. The visual notation reduces misinterpretation compared to natural language descriptions.
Documentation: UML creates permanent documentation of system design that remains valuable throughout the software lifecycle. When systems require maintenance or enhancement years after initial development, UML diagrams provide crucial insight into original design decisions and system organization.
Communication: UML bridges communication gaps between technical and non-technical stakeholders. Business users can validate use case diagrams representing system functionality even without programming knowledge, while developers gain precise technical specifications from class and sequence diagrams.
UML Diagram Categories
UML encompasses numerous diagram types, broadly categorized as structural diagrams and behavioral diagrams:
Structural Diagrams represent the static architecture of systems:
* Class Diagrams show object-oriented classes, their attributes, methods, and relationships
* Component Diagrams illustrate high-level software components and their dependencies
* Deployment Diagrams depict how software is distributed across hardware infrastructure
* Package Diagrams organize related classes into logical groupings
Behavioral Diagrams represent dynamic system behavior:
* Use Case Diagrams capture functional requirements from user perspectives
* Sequence Diagrams show how objects interact over time through message exchanges
* Activity Diagrams represent workflows and business processes
* State Machine Diagrams model how objects transition between different states
For ProTrackAI's design documentation, this chapter focuses on five essential diagram types that comprehensively capture the system's requirements and architecture: use case diagrams for functional requirements, class diagrams for object-oriented structure, sequence diagrams for component interactions, activity diagrams for process workflows, and database diagrams for data persistence design.
UML in Agile Development
While UML originated in traditional waterfall development methodologies, it remains valuable in modern agile contexts. Rather than creating exhaustive upfront documentation, agile teams use UML diagrams selectively to clarify complex aspects of system design, communicate architectural decisions, and onboard new team members. ProTrackAI's UML documentation strikes this balance—providing sufficient detail to guide implementation while avoiding excessive documentation overhead.
